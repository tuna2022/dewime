    columns:
      title: "Stained Glass: Technical Infrastructure and Craftsmanship"
      items:
        - title: "1. Sketch and Cartoon (Maquette & Cartoon)"
          text:
            - "• First, a scaled design (sketch) is prepared."
            - "• Next, a full-scale technical drawing, known as a \"cartoon,\" is produced."
            - "• On this cartoon, the color for each piece of glass is determined, and the lines (allowances) where the lead cames will be placed are drawn with millimeter precision. Using specialized double-bladed pattern shears, the paper is cut away to account for the space the lead will occupy."
        - title: "2. Glass Selection and Cutting"
          text:
            - "• Depending on the design, antique glass, cathedral glass, or opalescent glass is selected."
            - "• The artisan uses a diamond-tipped glass cutter to cut the glass, following the templates. For curved lines, any rough edges remaining after cutting are trimmed away using grozing pliers."
        - title: "3. Painting and Firing (Grisaille Technique)"
          text:
            - "• In classic stained glass, details such as facial expressions, folds in clothing, or shadows are applied to the glass using a special paint called \"Grisaille\"—a mixture of iron oxide and finely ground glass powder."
            - "• The painted glass pieces are then fired in a kiln at temperatures between approximately 600°C and 650°C. At this temperature, the paint melts into the surface of the glass, becoming permanently fused with it."
        - title: "4. Leading and Soldering"
          text:
            - "• The cut and painted pieces are individually fitted into the channels of soft, H-profile lead strips (cames)."
            - "• Once all components have been assembled and compressed, the joints of the lead cames are fused together using a soldering iron and a 60/40 tin-lead alloy solder. To ensure the structural integrity of the panel, soldering is applied to both the front and back surfaces."
        - title: "5. Cementing (Waterproofing)"
          text:
            - "• To prevent the glass pieces from rattling when the stained glass panel is positioned upright, to ensure it can withstand wind pressure, and to make it watertight, a specialized stained glass putty—composed of chalk powder, linseed oil, and drying agents—is worked into the gaps between the lead cames and the glass using brushes."
            - "• Following the cleaning process, the panel is left to \"mature\" (cure) until it reaches the necessary strength for installation into the structure."
