A former factory offers something few conventional homes can: the freedom to inhabit a space at an entirely different scale. High ceilings, broad windows, exposed brick and steel columns recall the building's working life, while open floors allow living, dining and creative work to flow together.

When artists and young residents began moving into these buildings, they brought more than furniture. Large walls became places for ambitious paintings; sculptural objects found room to breathe; collected pieces gave each interior a distinct point of view. Art was woven into everyday life, visible from the kitchen table as readily as from the sofa.

The most compelling conversions preserve that sense of discovery. A worn concrete floor can sit beside a carefully chosen rug. A steel beam can frame a quiet reading corner. Warm light and personal objects soften the scale without erasing the building's history. What emerges is a home with both openness and intimacy, shaped by the people who live there and the work that came before them.
